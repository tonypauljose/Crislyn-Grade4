The three-week plan

Twenty-five minutes a day beats three hours on the Sunday before. This is the shape of it — the app handles the detail.

Week 1 — meet everything

Three or four lessons a day, each one straight into practice, plus review of what came before. Expect mistakes; that is the point. Nothing is marked "mastered" yet.

Week 2 — close the gaps

Reviews start returning on their own. Use Trouble spots on the two weakest subjects. First mock paper on paper at the weekend.

Week 3 — make it automatic

Mostly review now. Tables and Roman numerals daily for speed. Second mock paper, timed. Aim for every skill at ◕ or ●.

The last two days

No new work. One Trouble spots round per subject, read the written answers aloud, sleep early.

How the app decides what she practises

1. Taught before tested. A skill she has never been shown is never sprung on her as a question. The first time one comes up, the lesson runs — the method, one example worked out a step at a time, and the mistake to watch for — and only then does the drill ask her anything. Lessons are paced the same way the questions are, so the whole portion is taught with about five days spare.

2. Retrieval, not re-reading. Every turn asks her to produce the answer. Recalling something is what makes it stick; reading it again mostly makes it feel familiar.

3. Spaced repetition. Each skill sits in a box. Get it right and it comes back in 1, then 2, 4, 7, then 12 days. Get it wrong and it drops back and returns tomorrow. The intervals are squeezed to fit the weeks left before the exam.

4. Mastery, not attendance. A skill only turns gold after six correct recalls spread over at least three different days, with three right in a row. One lucky answer proves nothing.

5. Kept at the right difficulty. Three in a row and the questions get harder; two slips and they ease off. Around 70–80% correct is the sweet spot — a quiz she gets 100% on is not teaching her anything.

6. Interleaved. The daily mix jumps between subjects and topics on purpose, so she has to work out which method to use, not just repeat one.

7. Mistakes come back. A missed question re-enters the same session a few questions later with different numbers — not immediately, when she could just copy.
